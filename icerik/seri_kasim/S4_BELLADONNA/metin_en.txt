In Renaissance Italy, women put poison in their eyes... to look beautiful!
The plant is called belladonna. Italian for "beautiful lady".
You might know its other name: deadly nightshade. It still grows wild across Britain.
A few drops in the eyes... and the pupils opened wide.
Back then, that meant a deep, seductive gaze.
The price? Blurry vision, a racing heart... and every part of the plant is poisonous.
In 1753, Carl Linnaeus gave it a scientific name: Atropa.
After Atropos, the Greek Fate who cuts the thread of life!
So its name means "beautiful lady"... and "death" at the same time.
