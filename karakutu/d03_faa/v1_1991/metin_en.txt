November 13, 2026. With thirty-five hundred planes in the air, controllers suddenly couldn't hear their pilots.
Seventeen of twenty-two control centers went dark... and the FAA stopped every departure in the country!
No hacker. No attack. A single new router in Arkansas flooded the network with connection attempts.
But here's the real shock... it had happened before, thirty-five years ago!
September 17, 1991, New York. A power failure hit an AT&T switching center in Manhattan.
Air traffic ran over those phone lines. Controllers at Kennedy, LaGuardia and Newark lost their pilots.
Three airports shut down for hours. Thousands were stranded in terminals and on planes sitting on the runway.
The fix? A backup network of microwave dishes, built right alongside the phone lines.
Thirty-five years later, copper became fiber... but the weak spot never moved: one single box!
