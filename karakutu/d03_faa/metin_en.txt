Five days... three massive crashes! In America, first email went down, then the planes, then the banks.
November 12. Microsoft's Outlook, Teams and Copilot crashed worldwide.
The cause: a technical failure, the company says, not a cyberattack. The result: businesses and government offices were disrupted for two days.
November 13. With thirty-five hundred planes in the air, controllers suddenly couldn't hear their pilots.
The cause: a new router, installed in Arkansas during a copper-to-fiber upgrade, flooded the network with a thousand log-on attempts every four to five seconds.
The result: seventeen of twenty-two centers were hit, every departure stopped, and over twelve thousand flights were canceled that day.
November 16. This time, the banks. Chase got close to twenty thousand outage reports.
The cause: a technical glitch, the bank says. The result: logins, balances and Zelle transfers froze, and the problem reached the branches.
So, was it an attack? All three said no. And no link between them was found.
The common lesson: these systems hang on a single point, and they break most when they're being upgraded. The Transportation Secretary asked Congress for fifty billion dollars.
The wires are getting replaced... but one single box can still stop a country. Follow Black Box Files for the next case.
